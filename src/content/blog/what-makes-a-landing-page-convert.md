A landing page has one job: get a visitor to take a single action. That might be booking a call, joining a waiting list or buying a product. Everything on the page should help with that job, and anything that doesn't is a distraction.

Here is what I look for on every landing page I build.

## 1. A headline that says what you do

Visitors decide within seconds whether to stay. If your headline is clever but vague, they leave. Say what you offer and who it is for, in plain words.

Compare "Reimagine your workflow" with "Invoicing software for freelancers". The second one is less exciting, and it works better, because the right person knows immediately that they are in the right place.

## 2. One clear action

Pick one thing you want people to do and repeat that button down the page. Two competing buttons, such as "Buy now" and "Book a demo", split attention and lower the chance that anyone clicks either.

Secondary links are fine. Give them less visual weight than the main button.

## 3. Proof

People trust other people more than they trust your own claims. Real reviews, recognisable client names, numbers you can back up, or a short case study all help.

If you are just starting out and have none of these yet, do not invent them. Show the work itself instead: a demo, a sample, a clear explanation of your process.

## 4. Speed

A slow page loses visitors before they have read a word, especially on a phone over mobile data. Compress your images, avoid heavy plugins, and test the page on a real phone, not just your office Wi-Fi.

## 5. A design that works on a phone first

For many businesses, most visitors now arrive on a phone. Design for the small screen first: large tap targets, short paragraphs, and the main button visible without scrolling.

## Common mistakes

- **Too many choices.** A full navigation menu invites people to wander off.
- **Walls of text.** Short sections with clear headings are easier to scan.
- **Asking for too much.** Every extra form field costs you sign-ups. Ask only for what you need.
- **No next step.** If someone reaches the bottom of the page, give them the button again.

A good landing page is not complicated. It is focused. Decide the one thing you want, then remove everything that does not serve it.
