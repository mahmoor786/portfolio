The projects that go smoothly usually have one thing in common: the client had thought through a few basic questions before the first conversation. You do not need technical knowledge to answer them, and having the answers ready makes every quote you receive more accurate.

## 1. What is the website for?

"We need a website" is a starting point, not a goal. Do you want more enquiries, online sales, bookings, or simply somewhere credible to send people? The answer shapes every decision that follows.

## 2. Who is it for?

Describe your ideal customer in a sentence or two. A site aimed at busy restaurant owners should look and read differently from one aimed at teenagers buying trainers.

## 3. What should a visitor do?

Pick the single most important action: call you, fill in a form, buy, or book. A site designed around one clear action performs better than one that tries to do everything.

## 4. What pages do you need?

Most small business sites need fewer pages than people expect. Home, services, about and contact cover a lot. Write a rough list; your developer can help you trim or extend it.

## 5. Who will supply the content?

Text and photos are the most common cause of delays. Decide early whether you will write the words yourself, and whether you have good photos or need them taken.

## 6. Which websites do you like?

Collect three or four sites you admire and note what you like about each one: the colours, the layout, how easy it is to use. This tells a designer more than any description.

## 7. What is your budget and deadline?

Be open about both. A good developer will tell you what is realistic and suggest where to simplify if the two do not match. Hiding your budget usually leads to a quote that misses in one direction or the other.

## One more thing: who owns it?

Ask who will own the domain name, the hosting account and the code when the project ends. The answer should be you.

If you can answer these seven questions, you are ready to talk to a developer, and you will get far more useful replies when you do.
