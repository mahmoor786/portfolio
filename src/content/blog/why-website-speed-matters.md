It is tempting to judge a website by how it looks on the designer's large screen. Your customers judge it differently: by how quickly it appears on their phone while they are waiting for a bus.

## Slow pages lose people

If a page takes too long to show anything, many visitors simply go back and tap the next result. They never see your design, your offer or your prices. Every second of delay costs you some of the people you worked hard to attract.

## Search engines notice too

Google measures how fast pages load and how stable they are while loading, and it uses those measurements as one of many signals when ranking results. A fast site will not rank on speed alone, but a slow one starts at a disadvantage.

## What usually makes a site slow

- **Oversized images.** A photo straight from a camera can be twenty times larger than it needs to be.
- **Too many plugins and scripts.** Each chat widget, tracker and slider adds weight.
- **Cheap or overloaded hosting.** If the server is slow to respond, nothing else can fix it.
- **Heavy effects loaded up front.** Animation and 3D are fine when they load after the content, not before it.

## How to check your own site

Open Google's free PageSpeed Insights tool, paste in your address and run the test for mobile. You will get a score and a list of the biggest problems, usually with images at the top.

Then do the simplest test of all: open your site on your own phone, with Wi-Fi turned off. If you find yourself waiting, so are your customers.

## Fast and beautiful are not opposites

A well-built site can be both. The trick is in the order things load: text and layout first, so the page is readable immediately, then images, then any animation. Done properly, visitors get the impressive version without ever waiting for it.
